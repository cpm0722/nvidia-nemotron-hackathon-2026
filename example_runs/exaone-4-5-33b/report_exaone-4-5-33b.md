# Exaone 4.5 33B

## Executive Summary
Exaone 4.5 33B is LG AI Research's first open‑weight vision‑language model, featuring 33 B parameters (31.7 B language + 1.29 B vision encoder) and a 262 K token context length. [^1] According to the model card, it delivers competitive performance on general benchmarks while outperforming similarly sized SOTA models in document understanding and Korean reasoning tasks. [^1] Reddit announcements highlight the model’s release, noting its non‑commercial EXAONE 1.2 license and the need for forked vLLM/SGLang Transformers to run it. [^3][^4] Early users praise its strong reasoning scores (e.g., AIME 2025 92.9%) and vision capabilities, though some note the licensing restriction and that models like Qwen 3.5 27B remain competitive. [^3][^4]

## Official Performance
The model card reports EXAONE 4.5 33B scoring 78.7 on MMMU and 68.6 on MMMU‑Pro, placing it close to GPT‑5 mini (79.0/67.3) and slightly behind Qwen3‑VL 235B Thinking (80.6/75.0). [^1] On MathVision it reaches 75.2, exceeding GPT‑5 mini (71.9) and Qwen3‑VL 32B Thinking (70.2). [^1] In document‑understanding tasks it scores 89.0 on AI2D, 63.2 on OCRBench v2 and 81.2 on OmniDocBench v1.5, outperforming GPT‑5 mini (88.2, 55.8, 77.0) and matching or exceeding Qwen3‑VL variants. [^1] For Korean reasoning it achieves 91.9 on KRETA, 80.1 on K‑Viscuit and 42.7 on KMMMU, comparable to Qwen3‑VL 235B Thinking (92.8, 83.9, 42.1) and ahead of GPT‑5 mini (94.8, 78.5, 42.6). [^1] On LiveCodeBench v6 it attains 81.4, and on MMLU‑Pro 83.3, both in line with the GPT‑5 mini and Qwen3‑VL 32B Thinking figures. [^1] Overall, the benchmarks show EXAONE 4.5 33B is competitive with GPT‑5 mini and trades blows with Qwen3‑VL 32B on vision tasks, while lagging behind the larger Qwen3‑VL 235B Thinking and K‑EXAONE 236B models. [^1]

## Community Reactions
The Reddit announcement post received 68 upvotes and sparked a discussion about the model’s architecture and licensing. [^3] Commenters noted the model’s dense 33B design, 262K token context, and the requirement to use forked vLLM/SGLang Transformers for inference. [^3] Several users praised the model’s strong reasoning performance, citing its AIME 2025 score of 92.9% and LiveCodeBench v6 result of 81.4% as evidence of competitiveness with GPT‑5 mini. [^3][^4] A recurring criticism was the non‑commercial EXAONE 1.2 license, with remarks such as “the license sucks” and “no reason to even look at this thing” when permissively licensed alternatives exist. [^4] Some participants argued that models like Qwen 3.5 27B still deliver better overall performance, describing them as the “reigning champ” despite EXAONE 4.5’s strong vision scores. [^4] Despite the license concerns, the community acknowledged the model’s solid document‑understanding numbers (e.g., AI2D 89.0) and expressed interest in experimenting with the provided FP8 and GGUF quantizations. [^4]

## Key Takeaways
- Verify the model’s licensing terms before considering any commercial deployment, as the EXAONE 1.2 license prohibits commercial use.  
- Expect strong performance on document‑understanding and Korean reasoning benchmarks, with AI2D at 89.0 and KRETA at 91.9.  
- Plan to use forked vLLM, SGLang, or TensorRT‑LLM builds, as mainline versions do not yet support EXAONE 4.5 out‑of‑the‑box.  
- Leverage the 262K token context for long‑context tasks, but note that enabling thinking mode adds latency and may be disabled for speed‑critical workloads.  
- Compare against Qwen 3.5 27B and GPT‑5 mini on your own benchmarks to determine real‑world suitability for vision‑language applications.
